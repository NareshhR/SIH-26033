## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
1. EXPLAINATION: Instead of farmers listing produce and hoping it sells, buyers commit first. Households, housing societies, hostels and canteens join weekly Demand Pools. FPOs harvest only against pooled orders. Produce is delivered on a fixed timetable to existing Fasal Points, which are Fair Price Shops, Common Service Centres and Anganwadis.

2. HOW IT ADDRESSES THE PROBLEM: Pre-committed demand removes gluts and unsold stock. Delivery through existing outlets cuts last-mile cost. Fewer intermediaries means a higher farmer share and lower consumer prices.

3. INNOVATION: Pull-based Demand Pools. Supply is planned from committed demand, not listings. AI forecasts pool size per locality and crop.
Fasal Points, with no new fleet or dark stores. There are roughly 5.4 lakh Fair Price Shops, over 99% with e-PoS devices, and they sit under the same ministry that posed this problem. A fixed-day “Farm Run” works like a bus timetable, and route optimization designs the timetable instead of dispatching on demand.
Glut Switch. When AI forecasts a surplus in a cluster, it triggers flash pools, institutional buyers, FPO processing and storage-backed credit. This targets the 2026 pattern where farmers dumped onions and destroyed potato crops while retail prices stayed high. It also gives the Dept. of Consumer Affairs an early-warning feed for Price Stabilisation Fund decisions.

METHODOLOGY: Pool opens → buyers commit → AI sizes harvest per FPO → farmers confirm → hub grading → Farm Run to Fasal Points → pickup and UPI escrow release → Glut Switch and data feed back into forecasts. A working MVP covers pool creation, a forecast demo for tomato, onion and potato, a timetable optimizer, and a dashboard.

## Feasibility and Viability
1. FEASIBILITY: The technology is mature. FPS automation and AgriStack are already in place. The pilot starts in one cluster and one city with willing FPS and FPO partners. Revenue is a small commission plus service fees to Fasal Points.

2. CHALLENGES:
  ·FPS and state approvals.
  ·Commitment drop-offs.
  ·Perishability.
  ·Digital literacy.

3. STRATEGIES:
  ·Begin with cooperative-run or willing FPS.
  ·Small refundable pool deposits.
  ·Hub grading and cut-off times.
  ·Voice UI and Kisan Mitra agents.

## Impact and Benefits
1. IMPACT: Farmers get assured buyers, a higher share and fast payment. Consumers get cheaper, fresher produce near home. Government gets real-time price and glut alerts.

2. BENEFITS:
  ·Economic: less distress selling and lower logistics cost.
  ·Social: extra income for FPS dealers and rural agents.
  ·Environmental: less food loss and fewer delivery kilometres.
